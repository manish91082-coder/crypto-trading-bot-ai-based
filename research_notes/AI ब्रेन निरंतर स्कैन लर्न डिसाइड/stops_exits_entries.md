# Market-aware automatic stop-loss / exit methods and entry/exit decision modeling (crypto, 5m–1D, focus 4H; Binance spot + USDT-M perps, long and short)

Status legend: [PEER] peer-reviewed journal; [WP] working paper or staff report; [PREPRINT] arXiv, not peer-reviewed; [ABSTRACT-ONLY] only the abstract or a secondary summary was read, not the full text; [PRACT] practitioner or vendor material, no study behind it; [DOCS] official exchange docs.
Research done 2026-10-17. About 19 tool calls. Several full texts could not be opened (MIT DSpace returned 429, Springer redirected to a login, Alpha Architect returned 403), so many items are tagged ABSTRACT-ONLY.

## Q1. Do stop-loss rules add value, and do volatility-scaled stops beat fixed-% stops? (Kaminski & Lo; Lo & Remorov; chandelier origins; crypto studies)

### Takeaway
The theory is clear. Under a random walk a stop-loss rule can only lower expected return. A stop adds value only when returns have momentum or serial correlation, or when the market switches between a calm bull regime and a volatile bear regime. Even then, tight stops lose to buy-and-hold after trading costs, and the downside-risk reduction is modest. I found **no peer-reviewed head-to-head test of ATR (volatility-scaled) stops against fixed-% stops**, in any asset or in crypto. The case for ATR stops rests on theory and practitioner lore. The crypto evidence is that *simple* stops reduced drawdowns and raised Sharpe in portfolio or momentum settings (small samples, 2017–2021 era).

### Cited Findings
- **Kaminski & Lo, "When do stop-loss rules stop losses?"**, Journal of Financial Markets 18 (March 2014): 234–254 [PEER, ABSTRACT-ONLY]. The paper gives an analytical framework for how stop-loss rules affect a portfolio's expected return and volatility. On daily index-futures data, some stop-loss policies raised expected return and substantially lowered volatility at longer sampling frequencies — [MIT DSpace record](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876); [author manuscript PDF](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/114876/Lo_When%20Do%20Stop-Loss.pdf)
- Earlier working-paper versions (2007–08) of Kaminski & Lo state two results. Under the Random Walk Hypothesis, simple 0/1 stop-loss rules *always reduce* expected return; under momentum they can add value. On monthly U.S. equity data 1950–2004, certain rules added roughly 50–100 bp/month during stop-out periods. These figures come from the WP drafts, not the published abstract — [Swedish House of Finance WP abstract](https://swopec.hhs.se/sifrwp/abs/sifrwp0063.htm); [dailyspeculations summary](https://dailyspeculations.com/wordpress/?p=14247)
- **Lo & Remorov (2017), "Stop-loss strategies with serial correlation, regime switching, and transaction costs"** (Journal of Financial Markets, 2017) [PEER, ABSTRACT-ONLY]. The paper derives closed-form stop-loss returns under serial correlation, regime switching and transaction costs. On a large sample of U.S. stocks, *tight* stop-loss rules underperform buy-and-hold in a mean-variance framework, mainly because of excessive trading costs. Outperformance is possible for stocks with sufficiently high serial correlation. Some strategies reduce downside risk, but not substantially — [MIT DSpace 1721.1/107017](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/107017); [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-premium/stop-losses-on-stock-positions-in-depth)
- A CXO summary of the 2015 WP version (daily data 1964–2014) gives two more results. Tight-stop performance is roughly linear in the asset's volatility and autocorrelation. *Wide* stops beat buy-and-hold for regime-switching assets when the bull regime has low volatility, the bear regime has high volatility, and switches are infrequent; the gain comes from lower downside volatility — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-premium/stop-losses-on-stock-positions-in-depth). One search summary claimed the studies were run on "Iranian markets". That is inconsistent with the MIT abstract (U.S. stocks), so I treat it as an aggregator error.
- **Han, Zhou & Zhu, "Taming Momentum Crashes: A Simple Stop-Loss Strategy"** [WP/SSRN, ABSTRACT-ONLY]. With a 10% daily stop on individual momentum legs, the worst monthly loss fell from about -49.79% to about -11.34% for the equal-weighted portfolio, and from about -65.34% to about -23.69% for value-weighted. Average returns and Sharpe ratios more than doubled. The numbers differ slightly between versions (2014 vs 2016) — [Crossing Wall Street (abstract repro)](https://www.crossingwallstreet.com/?p=28384); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2016/08/taming-the-momentum-roller-coaster-fact-or-fiction/). Critiques: the results are gross of costs, and trading frictions for stocks in stop-loss conditions can be extraordinarily high — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=25071). Stops add trading and expense — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2016/08/taming-the-momentum-roller-coaster-fact-or-fiction/) (full text was 403, not verified).
- **Crypto: Kaya & Mostowfi (2022), Finance Research Letters 46, 102422**, doi 10.1016/j.frl.2021.102422 [PEER]. Adding a simple stop-loss rule to low-volatility crypto portfolios "reduced markedly" the downside risk and improved Sharpe ratios significantly. Sample: liquid coins, Jan 2017–Jun 2021. This is a fixed stop, not a trailing or ATR stop — [ZHAW repository PDF](https://digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/bitstream/11475/23853/3/2022_Kaya-Mostowfi_Low-volatility-strategies-cryptocurrencies_frl.pdf)
- **Crypto: Sadaqat & Butt**, Journal of Behavioral and Experimental Finance [PEER, ABSTRACT-ONLY via search snippet]. A stop-loss momentum strategy across 147 cryptocurrencies reportedly produced higher returns, Sharpe and alphas than benchmark momentum strategies — [IBA summary page](https://sbs.iba.edu.pk/stop-loss-rules-momentum-payoffs-cryptocurrencies.php)
- **Crypto preprint arXiv 2604.27150**, "Optimal Stop-Loss and Take-Profit Parameterization for Autonomous Trading Agent Swarm" [PREPRINT, ABSTRACT-ONLY]. Over 900+ agent trades, the better configurations favored tighter loss limits, earlier profit capture and closer trailing protection. This is a small, single-system sample — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.27150)
- **Chandelier exit origin** [PRACT]. The stop hangs a multiple of ATR from the highest high since entry (longs) or the lowest low (shorts). It is credited to Chuck LeBeau and appears in LeBeau & Lucas, *Computer Analysis of the Futures Market(s)* (1992). It was popularized by Alexander Elder in *Come Into My Trading Room* (2002). The common default is 22 periods × 3 ATR. ATR itself comes from J. Welles Wilder (1978) — [NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/indicators/chandelier-exit); [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/chandelier-stop.md). Conflict: LuxAlgo says LeBeau introduced it in System Traders Club bulletins in the late 1990s, which clashes with the 1992 book date. Unresolved.
- **ATR vs fixed-%: practitioner material only** [PRACT]. Fixed-% stops get hit constantly in high-volatility regimes, while ATR stops scale with current conditions. Vendor claims such as "3×ATR improves performance 15%" or "22% lower drawdown" have no traceable study behind them and should not be used — [LuxAlgo ATR stop](https://www.luxalgo.com/blog/how-to-use-atr-for-volatility-based-stop-losses/); [Two Quants substack](https://twoquants.substack.com/p/volatility-vs-average-true-range). A critique says ATR trailing stops can move *down* during an uptrend if ATR widens, and whipsaw outside strong trends — [Incredible Charts](https://www.incrediblecharts.com/indicators/atr_average_true_range_trailing_stops.php)

### Inferences
- For crypto at 4H, stops can only help if there is serial correlation (trend persistence) or regime switching. Crypto plausibly has both, but the size of the gain must be measured *after costs* on our own data. Lo–Remorov's warning against *tight* stops applies directly.
- Lo–Remorov's "wide stop + regime switching" result is the strongest academic support for a wide, regime-aware stop (e.g., a wide chandelier). That is more defensible than a tight fixed-% stop.
- Our F7 result (short stop at entry ×1.5, 30-day cooldown, improved drawdown) fits the literature: stops mainly cut downside and drawdown rather than raise mean return. It should still be checked net of costs, with a shuffled or placebo control.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or OOS study found comparing ATR-scaled vs fixed-% stops head-to-head (any asset, or crypto). This must be tested internally with pre-registration.
- No peer-reviewed crypto study specifically on *trailing* stops was found.
- The Kaminski–Lo published full text (exact thresholds and numbers) was not opened (HTTP 429).

## Q2. Stop distance vs holding period and noise; optimal stop distance (in ATR multiples) and robustness

### Takeaway
Theory says stop performance scales with volatility and autocorrelation. Tight stops cost more through trading and whipsaw; wide stops help mainly in regime-switching markets. Optimal-stopping math (Leung & Zhang) gives model-specific optimal trailing-stop and limit-exit regions, but only under parametric models (exponential OU). I found **no robust, out-of-sample-validated "optimal ATR multiple"**. The 3×ATR / 22-bar chandelier default is convention, not evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Tight-stop performance is about linear in return volatility and autocorrelation. Tight stops underperform buy-and-hold due to costs — [CXO summary of Lo–Remorov](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-premium/stop-losses-on-stock-positions-in-depth); [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/107017)
- Kaminski & Lo: the stop-loss benefit depends on sampling frequency; benefits appeared at longer sampling frequencies — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876)
- **Leung & Zhang, "Optimal Trading with a Trailing Stop"**, Applied Mathematics & Optimization 83: 669–698 (doi 10.1007/s00245-019-09559-0; online 2019, issue 2021) [PEER]. The trailing stop is defined as the first time price drops below f(running max). The paper solves optimal double stopping (entry, then exit). It proves that pairing a sell *limit* (take-profit) order with the trailing stop is optimal, and illustrates this under exponential OU with sensitivity analysis — [arXiv 1701.03960](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.03960); [IBKR Campus summary by Leung](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/ibkr-quant-news/optimal-trading-with-a-trailing-stop/)
- The common chandelier default (22, 3×ATR) is convention — [NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/indicators/chandelier-exit)

### Inferences
- **Our internal swing statistics as a prior.** A confirmed swing has about 2 ATR left on average and ≥1 ATR more 75–78% of the time; a typical full swing is about 5 ATR. A trailing or chandelier stop tighter than about 1–1.5 ATR from the extreme would likely cut a large share of trades that still have room to run (whipsaw). A stop of about 3 ATR from the extreme would give back much of a 2-ATR remaining move. This implies a structural trade-off, not a free lunch. Any ATR multiple must be chosen on a pre-registered grid with walk-forward OOS and costs, and its robustness checked as a plateau rather than a peak.
- Leung–Zhang's "trailing stop + limit take-profit" optimality supports combining a TP target (e.g., near the remaining ~2 ATR) with a trailing stop. But that result is model-dependent (OU, mean-reverting), not a crypto-trend result.

### Gaps
- No empirical study found giving an OOS-robust optimal ATR multiple for crypto at 4H.
- No study found that relates stop width to holding period in crypto specifically.

## Q3. Structure-based (swing-point) stops, stop-hunting and liquidity clustering near obvious levels

### Takeaway
Stops placed just beyond swing lows or highs are **practitioner lore with no direct academic test found**. There is rigorous evidence of the *mechanism* that makes obvious levels dangerous. In FX, stop-loss orders cluster just beyond round numbers, and hitting those clusters produces fast, self-reinforcing price cascades (Osler). In Bitcoin, prices cluster strongly at round numbers (Urquhart). Crypto "liquidation-cluster sweep" claims are vendor or educational material and not quantified.

### Cited Findings
- **Osler, "Stop-loss orders and price cascades in currency markets"**, NY Fed Staff Report 150 (2002); published in Journal of International Money and Finance 24(2), 219–241 (March 2005) [PEER; read via WP abstract]. Stop-loss orders generate positive-feedback trading. High-frequency FX data show trends are unusually fast near levels where stop-loss orders cluster, and the response to stop-loss clusters is larger than to take-profit clusters. Cascades may help explain the fat tails of exchange-rate returns — [NY Fed SR150 PDF](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr150.pdf); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org:443/p/fip/fednsr/150.html)
- (From background knowledge, not re-verified in this session: Osler 2003, Journal of Finance, "Currency orders and exchange rate dynamics" reports that take-profit orders cluster at round numbers and stop-loss orders cluster just beyond them. Verify before citing.)
- **Urquhart (2017), "Price clustering in Bitcoin"**, Economics Letters 159: 145–148 [PEER, ABSTRACT-ONLY]. Over 10% of BTC prices end in 00 decimals. There is no significant return pattern after round numbers. Clustering is positively related to price and volume, consistent with the negotiation hypothesis — [Southampton eprints](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/412873); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecolet/v159y2017icp145-148.html)
- Follow-ups: whole-number clustering is strongest on Fridays (Mbanga 2019, Applied Economics Letters) — [Missouri State](https://bearworks.missouristate.edu/articles-cob/431). There is intraday clustering (Baig & Sabah 2019) — [AUT Applied Finance Letters](https://ojs.aut.ac.nz/applied-finance-letters/article/download/200/82). A sentiment link explains about 2.5–5% of abnormal clustering (FRL 2019) — [bepress](https://works.bepress.com/nasim-sabah/3)
- Crypto liquidation cascades and stop clustering at round numbers or swing highs/lows [PRACT]. Exchange explainers advise avoiding obvious levels and describe the cascade mechanics, without quantification — [BIT knowledge hub](https://www.bit.com/knowledge-hub/cascade-liquidation). A narrative example is the November episode in which about $1.9B was liquidated in 4 hours, 85% of it longs (not a formal study) — [Coinchange](https://www.coinchange.io/blog/bitcoins-2-billion-reckoning-how-novembers-liquidations-cascade-exposed-cryptos-structural-fragilities)

### Inferences
- If swing-based stops are used, place them with an ATR buffer beyond the swing *and* away from round numbers. Osler's FX evidence implies that the level just beyond an obvious point is where cascades and fast slippage happen. Expect worse fills there, not just a higher hit probability.
- Combining structure and volatility (swing point minus k×ATR) is a reasonable hypothesis to test, but there is no academic evidence it beats a pure ATR stop.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found testing swing-point stops vs ATR or fixed stops.
- No peer-reviewed crypto study found quantifying stop-hunting or liquidation-cluster sweeps near swing levels (only vendor heatmap claims, e.g., ">15% OI in a 2% band", which are unsupported).

## Q4. Exchange-side stop execution on Binance: stop-market vs stop-limit, slippage, mark vs last price triggers

### Takeaway
On **USDT-M futures**, conditional orders are now placed through the **Algo (conditional) order endpoint** and use `triggerPrice`. `workingType` chooses MARK_PRICE or CONTRACT_PRICE (last), and **the API default is CONTRACT_PRICE**, not mark. `priceProtect` blocks triggering when mark and last diverge beyond the symbol's threshold. The trailing stop `callbackRate` is 0.1–10 (%). On **spot**, STOP_LOSS (market) and STOP_LOSS_LIMIT exist, plus `trailingDelta` and OCO. Stop-market guarantees an exit but may slip; stop-limit controls price but may not fill in a gap.

### Cited Findings
- USD-M Futures [DOCS]: "Use this endpoint to place TP/SL (Take Profit / Stop Loss) and trailing stop orders". Under `algoType=CONDITIONAL` the supported types are STOP_MARKET, TAKE_PROFIT_MARKET, STOP, TAKE_PROFIT and TRAILING_STOP_MARKET. Algo orders use `triggerPrice`, not `stopPrice` — [Binance USD-M Futures Trade REST API](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/trade/rest-api)
- `workingType` is MARK_PRICE or CONTRACT_PRICE, "Default: CONTRACT_PRICE". The trigger condition for STOP/STOP_MARKET BUY is latest price (MARK_PRICE or CONTRACT_PRICE) >= triggerPrice (the SELL side mirrors it) — [Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/trade/rest-api)
- `priceProtect` is used with STOP_MARKET or TAKE_PROFIT_MARKET and defaults to false. When on, the trigger requires that the difference rate between MARK_PRICE and CONTRACT_PRICE not exceed the symbol's `triggerProtect` — [Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/trade/rest-api)
- TRAILING_STOP_MARKET: `callbackRate` min 0.1, max 10. `activatePrice` defaults to the latest price. A BUY trailing stop triggers when price rises callbackRate above the lowest price since placement. An order that would trigger immediately is rejected with -2021 "Order would immediately trigger." — [Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/trade/rest-api)
- `closePosition=true` (STOP_MARKET/TAKE_PROFIT_MARKET) closes the whole position and cannot be combined with quantity or reduceOnly. `reduceOnly` cannot be sent in Hedge Mode — [Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/trade/rest-api)
- In the Binance Futures UI, the trigger can be set to Last Price or Mark Price — [Binance FAQ](https://www.binance.com/de/support/faq/360033779452). A Binance Square community post claims mark price is the default for SL and liquidation in the UI; this conflicts with the API default (CONTRACT_PRICE) and is not an official doc — [Binance Square](https://www.binance.com/en/square/post/43296). Liquidation itself is mark-price-based (widely documented; not re-fetched here).
- Spot [DOCS]: STOP_LOSS and TAKE_PROFIT execute a MARKET order when triggered. STOP_LOSS_LIMIT and TAKE_PROFIT_LIMIT also need `price`. Triggers can use `stopPrice` and/or `trailingDelta` (with `trailingTime`). An OCO pairs a LIMIT_MAKER/TAKE_PROFIT(_LIMIT) leg with a STOP_LOSS(_LIMIT) leg. For SELL: TP price > Last Traded Price > stop stopPrice — [Binance Spot trading endpoints](https://developers.binance.com/docs/binance-spot-api-docs/rest-api/trading-endpoints). The page states the trigger reference only as "market price". The `trailingDelta` unit (believed to be BIPS) was not confirmed on the fetched page.
- Stop-market fills at the best available price and can slip past the stop in volatile markets; stop-limit may never fill if price gaps through the limit [PRACT] — [Binance FAQ](https://www.binance.com/de/support/faq/360033779452); [Bitsgap help](https://bitsgap.com/ja/helpdesk/article/21066287597340-Stop-Limit-Order-Futures-Trading)

### Inferences
- For a protective exit on perps, the robust choice is STOP_MARKET with `workingType=MARK_PRICE`, `reduceOnly` (one-way mode) or `closePosition=true`, and possibly `priceProtect=true`. Mark-price triggering avoids last-price wick spikes (stop hunts on thin prints). The cost is that the actual fill is still at market, so slippage remains. This must be set *explicitly*, because the API default is CONTRACT_PRICE.
- Note a design tension. `priceProtect` can *prevent* a stop from triggering during a mark/last divergence, i.e., exactly in a crash. For a must-exit stop, test the behavior on testnet before relying on it.
- Backtests should model stop fills as max(stop, next-bar open) with slippage (gap-through), not exactly at the stop price, especially near round numbers (Osler).

### Gaps
- I did not find a Binance doc that quantifies slippage of stop-market orders, or an official statement of the UI default trigger.
- The algo-order migration date and whether legacy `/fapi/v1/order` STOP types are still accepted were not confirmed; check the changelog.

## Q5. Triple-barrier labeling, meta-labeling, and RL exit policies: OOS, after-cost evidence (especially crypto) and critiques

### Takeaway
Triple-barrier labeling (volatility-scaled profit-take / stop-loss / time barriers; López de Prado, *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*, 2018) has **some peer-reviewed crypto evidence of positive after-cost OOS performance**: Financial Innovation 2025, BTC/ETH tick data with CUSUM filtering. I could not open the full text. Meta-labeling (a secondary model that filters and sizes a primary signal) has framework papers in the Journal of Financial Data Science, but I found **no independent crypto OOS after-cost validation** of meta-labeling. Deep-RL crypto trading papers mostly admit serious backtest-overfitting risk and use short OOS windows. I found **no credible RL *exit-timing* study in crypto with costs**.

### Cited Findings
- **Financial Innovation (2025), doi 10.1186/s40854-025-00866-w** [PEER, ABSTRACT-ONLY via search snippet; full text blocked by Springer login]. On BTC and ETH tick data from Jan 2018 to Jun 2023, CUSUM-filtered bars with Triple-Barrier labeling outperformed time bars and next-bar prediction. It achieved consistently positive trading performance after transaction costs. Whether meta-labeling was used, the barrier parameters and the cost level are unverified — [Springer link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40854-025-00866-w)
- A genetic-algorithm-tuned triple-barrier study for crypto pair trading trains on 2017–2022 and tests Sep 2022–Dec 2023 (Jeonbuk National Univ.) [PEER?, ABSTRACT-ONLY]. Cost treatment is unknown — [JBNU pure](https://pure.jbnu.ac.kr/en/publications/enhanced-genetic-algorithm-driven-triple-barrier-labeling-method-/)
- Practitioner warning [PRACT]: if the barrier's minimum return is below the true round-trip cost, the pipeline labels cost-driven noise as signal — [MQL5 article](https://www.mql5.com/es/articles/22372)
- **Meta-labeling.** It is credited to López de Prado (2017/2018) as a secondary model that judges whether to act on a primary signal (filtering false positives, sizing) — [Wikipedia: Meta-Labeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-Labeling). A Hudson & Thames series in the Journal of Financial Data Science (2022) includes "Meta-Labeling: Calibration and Position Sizing". Calibration noticeably improves fixed position-sizing methods, while sizing functions learned from training data gain little — [Hudson & Thames meta-labeling tag](https://hudsonthames.org/tag/meta-labeling/); [Wikipedia refs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-Labeling) [PEER, ABSTRACT-ONLY; author attribution to Joubert et al. inferred from Wikipedia and H&T, not verified on the JFDS page]
- **RL crypto, Gort et al., arXiv 2209.05559** [PREPRINT]. "Deep RL for Cryptocurrency Trading: Practical Approach to Address Backtest Overfitting" frames overfitting detection as a hypothesis test and discards agents with a high probability of overfitting. OOS is only May–June 2022 (10 coins, two crashes). Less-overfitted agents beat more-overfitted ones and the benchmarks. The OOS window is very short, and cost modeling in the reported results was not confirmed — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05559v1)
- An ensemble DRL crypto paper (arXiv 2309.00626) notes that final-epoch models can overfit and fail badly under regime shifts [PREPRINT] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.00626)
- Optimal stopping (analytic, not ML): Leung & Zhang derive optimal entry and exit under a trailing stop (exp-OU). These are model-based, not data-driven — [arXiv 1701.03960](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.03960)

### Inferences
- **Our failed AI exit brains (AUC 0.505–0.526) are consistent with the literature.** Exit timing on a per-bar "exit now?" target is close to predicting the next return sign, which is near-random at 4H. The literature that does show positive results changes the *formulation*. It uses event-based sampling (CUSUM), volatility-scaled barrier labels, and a primary/secondary (meta) split, rather than direct exit prediction. The sensible next step is not "a better exit classifier". It is:
  (1) a rule-based exit (chandelier / ATR trail + time stop + TP near the expected remaining ~2 ATR), with ML used only as a meta-label on the *entry* (take/skip, size);
  (2) triple-barrier labels with barriers set in ATR units that exceed round-trip cost by a margin;
  (3) evaluation against a shuffled-label control, consistent with our existing proof standard.
- The ≥1-ATR-continuation rate of 75–78% after swing confirmation means a base rate of about 0.75 for a 1-ATR-up vs 1-ATR-stop barrier, before costs. Any meta-label model must beat that base rate (calibrated, Brier/log-loss), not AUC alone.
- RL for exits should be treated as high overfitting risk. If tried, it needs combinatorial purged CV / probability-of-backtest-overfitting style controls (as in Gort et al.), realistic costs and funding, and a long OOS window covering multiple regimes.

### Gaps
- No independent, peer-reviewed crypto study found validating meta-labeling OOS after costs.
- No crypto RL *exit / stop-placement* study with costs and a multi-year OOS was found.
- Full text of the Financial Innovation 2025 paper (barrier multiples, cost level, Sharpe) was not accessible; numbers need verification before citing.
- Not covered due to the tool budget: time-stop and breakeven-move evidence, and partial take-profit evidence. **No academic source found in this session for breakeven moves or partial TPs**; they remain practitioner lore pending internal tests.
