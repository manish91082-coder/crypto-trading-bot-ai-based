## Step 5: reasoning

1. On this day the **level** mattered more than the bias. The bearish bias paid only until the round number (S1). Below 10,000 the sellers failed: the prior low of 9,825 was never touched, and the 15m candle closed back above 10k. That "sweep and reclaim" was the exact turn.
2. **The perfect entry (9,994.86) was a wick below a round number.** It can only be known after the 15m close back above, so the realistic entry is 0.83% worse (L1). Even so it gave 2.05% net.
3. A breakout of the morning range (L2) was late in a range-type Sunday and gave nothing.
4. **Sunday character:** volume 38% below the day before, and a 3.5% range that went **up and down**. Both sides traded: S1 short in the morning, L1 long after.

## Step 6: candidate formula (hypothesis)

**H2 "liquidity sweep and reclaim":**
- When a 15m candle's low goes below a key level (a round thousand, or the prior-day low, or the 7-day low) and the candle **closes back above** it, enter long at the next open after one more bullish 15m close.
- SL = sweep low − 0.25%, TP = 2R. The mirror rule applies for shorts above key highs.
- **H1 (from Day 1) and H2 can conflict.** Day 1's double top at 9,990 was the mirror case (a sweep above, followed by a fall). A combined rule is written after more days.
